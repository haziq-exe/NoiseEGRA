# What the paper is, and what each mechanism contributes to it

## The paper

Getting a small language model to write stories that a teacher could actually
use: they must obey a dozen checkable writing requirements pitched at a
middle-school reading level, and a class set of them must not all be the same
story. That is an applied problem in educational NLP, and the contribution is
judged on whether it makes that easier, cheaper or better than the alternatives.

Everything below is ranked against that, not against how interesting the
mechanism is.

## What the alternatives actually are

**Fine-tuning, which is the state of the art for this exact task.**
[Children's English Reading Story Generation via Supervised Fine-Tuning of
Compact LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bea-1.51/) (BEA 2026) generates K–2
reading stories under phoneme and readability constraints. Their recipe: 2,580
stories generated by GPT-4o and Llama-3.3-70B, then QLoRA fine-tuning of three
8B models, with a reward-weighted loss over five metrics. It works — Spache
readability 2.34–2.56 against a target below 3.0, toxicity near zero.

Three things about it matter for us. It needs **a teacher model and a training
corpus**, which most people deploying a classroom tool do not have. Its own
**diversity results are poor and they say so**: global self-BLEU 0.32–0.42, with
recurring character names (Sam, Pam), recurring animals (pigs) and recurring
settings (farms, Ohio, Illinois), which they attribute to the teacher model's
patterns. And they flag a **circularity risk** — the rewarded variant optimises
the same five metrics it is evaluated on.

**Prompting alone, which is the cheap alternative.** [On the Automatic
Generation and Simplification of Children's
Stories](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.218/) (EMNLP 2023) finds LLMs
"do not yet possess the ability to limit their vocabulary to levels appropriate
for younger age groups". Asking nicely is not enough; that is why a control
mechanism is needed at all.

**Decoding, which is what we benchmark against.** [Trading Off Diversity and
Quality in Natural Language Generation](https://aclanthology.org/2021.humeval-1.3/)
(HumEval 2021) establishes the quality–diversity tradeoff empirically across the
whole spectrum and finds nucleus sampling best when quality matters. This is
important for us: **the frontier we spent days mapping is the known tradeoff.**
We did not discover it. What we can claim is a different and better position on
it, not an escape from it.

**Steering, which is what our method is.** Two published limits bear directly on
us. [Style Vectors for Steering Generative LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.52/)
(EACL Findings 2024) establishes steering from recorded activations for style.
[On the Limits of Steering Vectors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01802) finds that
**composing several steering vectors causes interference** and that strong
steering **reduces output diversity**, producing homogeneous generations.

Those two findings are the reason this project exists, whether or not the
original paper framed it that way. Our task needs *twelve* requirements steered
at once, which is squarely in the interference regime, and steering costs
diversity, which is exactly what the perturbation is for.

## Ranked by what they contribute to that paper

### 1. Composing many constraint directions without interference

**What.** Twelve requirements, four to six steered directions, made mutually
orthogonal by Löwdin and summed to a **fixed total budget**, so adding a
direction redistributes strength instead of pushing harder.

**Result.** 2.75–3.06 requirements broken of twelve against 3.92 for
raised-temperature nucleus sampling, 3.95 for top-k and 4.30 for the untouched
model, on intervals well clear of zero, at 190–193 of 200 stories coherent.
Holds at every displacement setting and survived every rescoring.

**Why it is a contribution.** The steering-limits paper says composition
interferes; we compose six and measure that a *fixed budget* is what makes it
work — we have the ablation showing three directions collapse coherence to 83%
because the same budget is divided fewer ways, and that raising the total to 4
is much worse than 2. That is a concrete answer to a known limitation, on a real
task, at inference time with no training data and no teacher model.

**Against the alternative.** The SFT approach needs 2,580 GPT-4o stories and
QLoRA on an 8B model. Ours needs twelve hand-written contrast pairs per
requirement and runs on a 1.7B model at inference. That is the applied argument.

### 2. Restoring the diversity that steering costs

**What.** One perturbation per story, drawn from the subspace the model's *own*
stories differ along, projected clear of the constraint directions, applied at
the prompt.

**Result.** Both variety measures beaten against nucleus sampling from 1.75
stories out: what happens +3.2 [+0.5, +5.8], wording +2.7 [+1.0, +4.6], at 178
of 200 coherent. Against the untouched model the gap is far larger.

**Why it is a contribution.** This is the direct answer to the published finding
that steering homogenises output, and it is the axis the closest competitor
reports failing on — their stories repeat names, animals and settings. Drawing
the perturbation from the model's own between-story variation, rather than as
isotropic noise, is the part that is genuinely ours: isotropic noise at a
magnitude that changes anything breaks the text, which we measured.

**What is missing.** We have never run **the push with no perturbation** on this
task. Without it we cannot show the diversity cost of steering and therefore
cannot show we recovered it — which is the whole argument. This is the single
most important missing experiment and it is one condition.

### 3. The perturbation's size expressed in units of the data

**What.** Size measured against how far the model's own stories sit from their
average, rather than against the hidden state's norm. On this model a story sits
6.9 from the average at layer 6, and the size everything was run at works out at
15.0 — twice as far out as any story the model wrote.

**Why it matters.** It makes the method reproducible on a new model without a
blind sweep, and it makes "how hard are you perturbing?" answerable. A methods
subsection, not a headline, but the paper is weaker without it.

### 4. The frontier, reported as a frontier

**What.** Coherence and content diversity trade against each other through the
perturbation size, and the position is controllable: 190/200 with compliance won
and diversity level, through to 170/200 with both diversity measures won.

**Why it is a contribution, modestly.** It is the known quality–diversity
tradeoff, so the honest claim is *our knob moves along it differently from
temperature* — we get compliance that temperature cannot buy at any setting.
Presenting the curve is better science than picking the flattering point, but it
is not a discovery.

### 5. Reading-level control as a steered requirement

The reading floor is one of the twelve and is steered like the others. Grade
5.2–6.7 against the baselines' 3.4–3.6 at the same nominal task. Worth a
paragraph because grade-level fidelity is the applied point of the whole
exercise, and it is the axis the Arabic workshop paper was built on.

## Tier 2 — supporting analysis, one paragraph each

**The steered requirement set caps content diversity.** Our stories' commonest
words were *scent*, *air*, *sun*; the baseline's included *backpack*, *window*,
*stand*. Nothing in twelve requirements asked for anything to *happen*. Adding a
direction contrasting an event with a description moved the commonest words to
*run*, *grab*, *hit*, and won content diversity where perturbation tuning had
only tied. Useful because it says something general: **steering a set of purely
stylistic requirements puts a ceiling on how different the stories can be.**

**Where in the prompt the perturbation sits decides which diversity you get.**
Late-prompt perturbation varies wording; early-prompt varies content. A tidy
analysis figure.

**The register shield.** Extracting a direction purely to *forbid* it, rather
than to push it, removes refusals and planning-monologue entirely at small
perturbations. Honest limit: it does not generalise — a purpose-built direction
for assistant helpfulness moved that failure 23%→20%, i.e. nothing.

## Tier 3 — negative results, one line each in a limitations table

Per-story gain on a steered direction (inert, tested twice); aiming the
perturbation at one of the model's own sampled stories (appeared to be the
breakthrough, was measurement artefact); calibrating and shortening toward risky
anchors (works, heavily fitted to this task); a stronger total push; wider and
better-aimed shields; per-token noise (best compliance of anything at 1.67, and
*negative* diversity — worth one line because it is the published method's own
mechanism); entropy gating; wandering aim; and eight more.

## The experiments this paper still needs

1. **Push with no perturbation**, 200 stories. Without it there is no evidence
   that steering costs diversity and no evidence we restored it. One condition.
2. **Published decoders as baselines** — running: typical
   ([TACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.tacl-1.31/)), η-sampling
   ([EMNLP Findings 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.249/)),
   min-p ([ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082)), contrastive search
   ([NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06417)). Nucleus and top-k alone
   are not a sufficient decoding comparison in 2026.
3. **A second model.** Everything is Qwen3-1.7B. Qwen3-8B is running but T4s
   make it expensive; at minimum the compliance result should replicate.
4. **Human or LLM-judge quality rating.** Every number here is automatic. The
   BEA paper lists the absence of human evaluation as its first limitation and a
   reviewer will apply the same standard to us.
